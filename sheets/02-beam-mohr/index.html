Method

Beam

The reactions come from a finite element model of the beam: 240 Euler Bernoulli beam elements plus extra nodes at every support and load point. Point loads and moments go straight onto nodes. Distributed loads (uniform or linearly varying) use consistent nodal loads, so the nodal deflections and the reactions are exact for these load types, even for the statically indeterminate cases.

Once the reactions are known, V and M come straight from statics on a free body cut at each x. That makes the diagrams exact, including the jumps at point loads and point moments. The sheet also compares the finite element member end moments with the statics moments at every node; they agree to round off.

V(x) = Σ upward forces left of x M(x) = Σ F_i (x − x_i) − Σ C_j (C_j counterclockwise couples left of x) dV/dx = −q(x) dM/dx = V(x) EI v'' = M(x)

Signs: loads are positive downward, couples positive counterclockwise, M positive when it sags the beam, deflection positive upward. Shear and moment are drawn above the axis when positive.

Mohr's circle

For plane stress, rotating the element by θ (counterclockwise) gives:

σx' = (σx + σy)/2 + (σx − σy)/2 cos 2θ + τxy sin 2θ τx'y' = −(σx − σy)/2 sin 2θ + τxy cos 2θ center C = (σx + σy)/2 radius R = √(((σx − σy)/2)² + τxy²) σ1,2 = C ± R τmax = R tan 2θp = 2τxy / (σx − σy)

Convention used everywhere on this sheet: σ is plotted to the right and τ is plotted positive downward. The point X is (σx, τxy). With this choice, rotating the element counterclockwise by θ moves the point counterclockwise around the circle by 2θ. θp is measured counterclockwise from the x axis to the σ1 direction.

The absolute maximum shear treats the third principal stress as σ3 = 0 (plane stress), so it is half the spread of σ1, σ2 and 0. It is bigger than the in plane τmax whenever σ1 and σ2 have the same sign.

Limits: linear elastic, small deflections, no shear deformation in the beam.
