Method

Honesty note. This is a 2D, low Reynolds number lattice Boltzmann model for building intuition. It is not a substitute for real CFD, it does not model turbulence, and 3D effects are missing.

The lattice Boltzmann method tracks particle distributions f_i moving along 9 lattice directions (D2Q9). Every step they stream to the neighbor cell and then relax toward a local equilibrium. The collision is TRT (two relaxation times): the even part of each pair of opposite directions relaxes with τ, which sets the viscosity, and the odd part with a second rate picked so that Λ = 1/4. Plain BGK went unstable at Re = 100 at the benchmarked inlet speed (τ close to 0.51), and TRT stays stable there. Density and velocity are sums over the 9 directions.

f_i(x + c_i, t + 1) = f_i − (f_i⁺ − f_i^eq⁺) / τ − (f_i⁻ − f_i^eq⁻) / τ⁻ f_i⁺ = (f_i + f_ī) / 2 f_i⁻ = (f_i − f_ī) / 2 (τ − ½)(τ⁻ − ½) = Λ = 1/4 f_i^eq = w_i ρ (1 + 3 c_i·u + 4.5 (c_i·u)² − 1.5 u·u) ν = (τ − 1/2) / 3 Re = U L / ν p = ρ / 3

Boundaries: a Zou and He velocity inlet on the left. On the right, an outflow that holds the density at 1 and copies the non equilibrium part of the distributions from the column before (Guo, Zheng and Shi, 2002); a plain zero gradient copy let mass pile up in the channel until it blew up, so I swapped it. The tunnel walls use halfway bounce back. In open mode the top and bottom are periodic. Preset shapes use interpolated bounce back (Bouzidi, Firdaouss and Lallemand, 2001): each link that crosses the surface knows where the true wall is, so a circle behaves like a circle and not like a staircase. Drawn shapes have no exact outline, so they use plain halfway bounce back. The force on the obstacle comes from momentum exchange: every population that bounces off the obstacle hands it the momentum c_i (f_out + f_back).

Cd = 2 Fx / (ρ U² L) Cl = 2 Fy / (ρ U² L) St = f L / U

U is the inlet speed and L is the obstacle size: cylinder diameter or square side (10, 16 or 24 cells by grid), plate length (twice that), airfoil chord (three times that), or the height of a drawn shape. In tunnel mode the inflow is the fully developed parabolic channel profile (as in the Schäfer and Turek benchmark), and U is its mean speed; in open mode the inflow is uniform. The default peak lattice inlet speed is 0.05, the setting the benchmark passed with. A faster option (0.1) is offered and labeled as lower accuracy. τ comes from the Reynolds number; settings that would push τ below 0.51 are refused. Averages of Cd and Cl, and the Strouhal number, only use steps after a warm-up of 2 flow-through times (domain length divided by U), so the startup transient is left out; until then the sheet says "warming up". Strouhal number comes from the zero crossings of the lift signal once the period repeats to within 5 percent; otherwise the flow is reported as steady. At Re of 40 and above, the flow starts with a small, short-lived asymmetric kick: a transverse velocity bump of 0.3 U (Gaussian, one size L wide) just behind the obstacle at t = 0, which breaks the symmetry so shedding starts sooner. This follows the practice of starting shedding with a brief imposed perturbation (Braza, Chassaing and Ha Minh, 1986) or a small rotational field at t = 0 (Alfonsi and Giorgini, 1991). It decays away and does not change the time averaged result; the Schäfer and Turek check does not use it. The NACA 4 digit shape uses the standard thickness and camber equations, rotated about the quarter chord.

Checks: the Schäfer and Turek 2D-1 cylinder (Re = 20) gives Cd inside 5.5 to 5.8 at 20 cells per diameter (peak lattice inlet speed 0.05, the same as the sheet's default; the fine grid has 24 cells per diameter, the medium grid 16), a channel develops the parabolic Poiseuille profile, an open cylinder at Re = 100 (blockage 1/12, inlet speed 0.05) sheds with a Strouhal number between 0.15 and 0.18, and a closed box keeps its mass.
