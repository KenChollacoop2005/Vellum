Method

This is the direct stiffness method. Each member gets a small stiffness matrix in its own axes, gets rotated into global x and y, and gets added into one big matrix K for the whole structure. Then I solve K u = F for the node displacements u and work back to member forces.

A frame member has three degrees of freedom at each end (u, v and rotation θ) and uses Euler Bernoulli beam theory. A truss member only carries axial force, so it only has the EA/L terms.

A uniform load w on a frame member turns into the consistent nodal loads wL/2 and wL²/12 at each end. On a truss member it is lumped as wL/2 to each node, since a truss member can't carry bending. A "global y" load acts per meter of member length. A "perpendicular" load acts normal to the member.

The structure matrix is banded, so nodes are renumbered with reverse Cuthill McKee to keep the band narrow and then solved with a banded Cholesky factorization. If a pivot goes to zero or negative, the structure is a mechanism and the sheet says so instead of printing nonsense. Rotations at nodes with only truss members are held, since nothing there resists them.

Signs: axial force is positive in tension. Moment is positive when it sags the member (tension on the member's local −y side). The deflected shape uses the exact cubic shape within each member, plus the extra sag from a uniform load.

Limits: linear elastic, small displacements, no shear deformation, no buckling check, members only connect at shared nodes.

References: Kassimali, Matrix Analysis of Structures; McGuire, Gallagher and Ziemian, Matrix Structural Analysis. Full list in docs/references.md. Benchmark checks: Verification sheet.
